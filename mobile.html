Custom Mobile Web Development

As of 2021, 47.59% of internet traffic is mobile & 48.88% is from desktop computers in the U.S.A. This data is recent, according to government statistics. Tablets only account for about 3%. It's surprising, desktop computers still have more traffic. However, between 9 A.M. & - 5:00 P.M., the percentage of mobile traffic is much higher, for the obvious reasons.

In any case, mobile internet traffic exerts a huge influence on every aspect of society today. Mobile phone is the sole or primary device for many people. Cellular websites are necessary for a huge percentage of internet users, but they are meant to be completely different than desktop websites.

In the past, our phones were much less sophisticated. It was common for web developers to duplicate desktop sites, simply stripping out much of the content. They designed minimized clones of desktop sites for mobile. Cell phones, desktop computers, pads & smart televisions are completely different. Unless, a website is specifically designed for just one device, basic sites should be developed to be seen equally well on all devices, sizes & browsers. Another words, to be device & browser compliant. In layman's terms, it should be legible & clear on a cell phone with a 4" inch screen or a 85" smart television. Each device is equipt with specific functionality, the site should accomodate this for proper viewing. Browsers are applications with which to access the internet, such as Chrome, Firefox, Safari and several others. Websites should look the same on all browsers as well.


Differences In Displays

On cell phones, screen orientations, sizes, inputs, resolutions etc., are only some differences that could limit the accesibility of sites, when comparing them to devices with large displays. Regardless of source codes, programming languages, design expertise or applications a mobile website should never be frustrating to view. The text, CTA (buttons) or graphics should be legible. Despite many zoom levels on cells, an improperly designed desktop (full size) website cannot be read comfortably. If the viewport is correct, the website will accomodate to each display.

Though there are many differences between desktop & mobile displays there are the basic, obvious ones. For example: smaller displays, slower processors, less bandwidth, touch input, small keyboards, limited multitasking, limited functionality, less precision etc. Even though most cell phones are equiped with many zoom levels, using fingers or even a stylus doesn't compare with the precision of a cursor from a mouse. Every aspect should be taken into consideration when designing a well developed website.
